## 4. The 2,706 drafts — a queue that never drained, not an abandoned campaign

`brandaro_pending_messages` today:

| status | rows | window | distinct campaigns | distinct phones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| failed | **2,696** | 2026-03-20 → 2026-06-15 | 87 | **219** |
| pending | 10 | 2026-03-30 → 2026-04-14 | 3 | 0 |
| sent | 6 | 2026-03-20 | 1 | 6 |

The audit's "2,706 pending" has since been reclassified: they are `failed`, not
awaiting approval. The decisive number is **219 distinct phone numbers across
2,696 rows** — a mean of 12 attempts per lead, top offenders at 61, 58, 58, 58, 57
attempts to the *same number*:

```
+17877258375  61 attempts  2026-04-14 → 2026-06-15
+18094737719  58
+16896985010  58
+18093333549  58
+17877238625  57
```

Every batch lands at 13:00–13:03 UTC, ~30 rows/day, one campaign per day. That is
a daily cron re-enqueueing the same unreached leads, failing, and re-enqueueing
them tomorrow. It stopped on 2026-06-15 — the cron stopped, the backlog did not
drain.

Content is cold outreach, not transactional:
> "Hi, I noticed Blondes Salon doesn't have a website and thought you might be
> missing out on potential clients who search online for salons in San Juan…"

**Answer to the question asked:** this is a stopped queue, not an abandoned
campaign — and it is the third loop-guard case. Nothing was sent, so no one was
spammed; but had the send path worked, 219 businesses would have received up to
61 identical cold pitches each. The right action is **not** to drain it. It is to
delete the failed backlog and, if Brandaro outreach restarts, put it behind
`reserve_sms_send` with a per-recipient lifetime cap.

### The one that must not wait for the migration

`brandaro-execute-calls` returned **HTTP 200** to an unauthenticated request
carrying `{"healthcheck":true}`, with body:

```json
{"calls_initiated":0,"calls_failed":10,"gate_blocked":0,"total_processed":10,"dry_run":false}
```

It did not reject the request — it **ran the batch**. `dry_run:false`,
10 leads pulled and dialled. The dials failed for unrelated reasons, and
`gate_blocked:0` means the suppression gate did not stop anything either.

This is `brandaro-call-twiml` again, one layer up: an unauthenticated caller can
trigger a real outbound dial batch. It needs the same treatment (auth gate at the
top, before any work) and it is the next thing to fix.
