## 4. Why the foreign number is the real finding

A STOP to `+18883022514` lands in a database we cannot read. Our `dnc_list` would
never learn about it, and `send-sms` would keep the number sendable. It is a second,
running copy of the exposure closed today — except this one we cannot audit, and its
inbound endpoint is unauthenticated.

**Not repointed.** If someone is on the other end of that project, cutting the
webhook breaks them with no error anyone sees.

## 5. Options, in order of least harm

1. **Register it first.** Insert a `dc_phone_numbers` row for all four unregistered
   numbers so the drift monitor stops treating them as invisible.
2. **Establish whether anyone is on the other side.** Zero SMS in 120 days and a
   self-dialing voice handler is strong evidence nobody is served by this today.
   Confirm with whoever stood up `clrgkreqqgmycrskcmwq`.
3. **Then, and only then:** either release the number (it has no traffic and we pay
   for it) or repoint SMS to `…/twilio-sms-webhook` so STOPs reach our suppression.
   Voice should not be repointed into the loop it currently is — it needs a real
   handler or a hangup, decided separately.

Cheapest interim step that breaks nothing: **repoint SMS only**, leave voice as-is.
Zero SMS traffic in 120 days means an SMS repoint cannot silently break a live flow,
and it closes the suppression blind spot immediately.
